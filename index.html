About meMy name is Annalise Balasubramanian. I mainly go by Annie, and I am a Toronto sailor racing the ILCA 6 for the Canadian Sailing Team and the Royal Canadian Yacht Club.

I’ve been on the water since I was a few months old. First aboard my parents’ boat, Amarone, then through the junior club program at the Island Yacht Club. In 2013, my family took a sabbatical aboard our family boat, Unknown Island, a Hanse 40, sailing from Toronto to the Caribbean and back with my sister and me homeschooled onboard. Once we got home, we joined the Royal Canadian Yacht Club where I learned to race Optis. I’ve been racing since 2015 and I’ve sailed ILCAs since 2018 (with a brief period in a C420, although sailing with a sibling at 13 years old is much more difficult than you think...).

I was a member of the Ontario Sailing Team from 2019 to 2023, and I’ve been on the Canadian Sailing Team since 2023. Now a full-time athlete on the senior national circuit, I’m campaigning for the LA 2028 and Brisbane 2032 Olympics, and hope to be for younger sailors what the older girls and ladies were for me.

Throughout my time as a youth sailor I qualified for three ISAF Youth Worlds - the U19 World Championships which happen every year, where only the top sailor per gender per class and per country qualifies to go. These regattas were a huge stepping stone for me. My first one, in Oman 2021, was the first international regatta I had done in an ILCA 6 as COVID had halted all travel. Sailing being an outdoor sport I made the most of it and kept training through the COVID years, even bundled in layers to get on the water as early as late March! That first summer during COVID I dislocated my shoulder one of the first times we were back on the water as a team. This set me back a few months. Rehabbing this injury and lots of hours in the gym kept things under control for a few years. The injury came up again at the 2024 Under 21 World Championships, where my shoulder dislocated rounding a top mark. I was able to finish the regatta in 15th, Canada’s best result at a U21 World Championship, and I was super proud of that despite the speed bump. Now it feels a little surreal to be out of the youth fleets, but I’m excited to hit the senior fleet running at full speed and ready to tackle anything that gets in my way.

While I’m not training or in my boat, I love to give back to the community through my sister’s not-for-profit organization, Whimsy Works.

Most of my volunteering with Whimsy Works has been at book banks and other fundraising events. At the book bank, I’ve dressed up as Anna alongside my sister as Elsa, and as Rapunzel for book readings and themed events! I’ve also had so much fun being Rapunzel (my favourite princess to be!!) at a Canada Day childhood cancer fundraiser with Donate4Kids. Another memorable event was when I went as Wonder Woman at Treat Accessibility - a community organization that makes Halloween accessible to every child. Putting a smile on these kids’ faces is more of a reward than standing on a podium!

With whatever free time I have at home, I love practicing my guitar, a hobby I’ve carried since I was 10 years old and picked up from my dad. I also love spending time with my family. Full family dinners are the norm, even with my sister and I (23 and 21) all over the city and world all the time. It’s a value that we have to be together as much as possible, probably something we picked up from spending so much time living on a 40-foot boat!

What is ILCA sailing?

The ILCA 6 is a one-person racing dinghy and the women’s Olympic sailing class. It is a one-design class, so everyone sails virtually identical boats, and the result comes down to the sailor: reading the wind, making decisions, handling the boat and executing under pressure. The boat is extremely physical, with hours of hiking hard over the side while constantly adjusting your body position, trimming your sail and steering.

In a regatta, there are usually three races a day over a four to six day racing period, depending on the regatta. Races last 40 to 60 minutes in anything from 5 to 30 knots. Scoring is low-point: 1 point for first, 2 points for second, and so on. Each sailor’s worst race gets discarded, so consistency matters the most. A World Championship runs for six days of racing. At the end of the five days, the top 10 sailors will race in what’s called a medal race on the sixth day: two short, high-intensity races where the points are condensed and anybody in the top 10 gets a shot at a medal. (The goal is to get into that top 10 😜)

A full-time campaign is year-round and worldwide, with World Championships, Grand Slams, North American Championships and Canadian Championships happening from January to December. Every racing venue is different, and it all comes down to reading the conditions and getting the most speed out of the boat.

Support

Back the campaign

An Olympic campaign comes with many costs. A new sail for every important regatta, new lines twice a season, coaching fees, accommodation and flights all add up over a four-year Olympic cycle.

I’m an Athlete Development Fund recipient from the RCYC and currently carded under Sport Canada, and there is still a lot that I self-fund in order to train at the highest capacity I can.

Donations through WindAthletes go towards coaching, travel, time on the water, and everything else I need to keep training. Any donation goes a long way, no matter how big or small.
